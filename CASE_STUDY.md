# HR Analytics – Employee Attrition Case Study

## 1. Background

Employee attrition is a major issue for companies because hiring and training new employees is costly and time-consuming.

This project analyzes employee data to understand why attrition happens and what factors influence it.

---

## 2. Problem

Companies often struggle to answer questions like:

* Why are employees leaving?
* Which departments have higher turnover?
* What factors increase the likelihood of attrition?
* Can we predict which employees might leave?

The goal of this analysis is to find data-backed answers to these questions.

---

## 3. Dataset

The dataset includes employee-related information such as:

* Age
* Department
* Monthly income
* Job role
* Job satisfaction
* Years at company
* Overtime status
* Attrition status (Yes/No)

Some values were cleaned and standardized before analysis.

---

## 4. Approach

### Data cleaning

* Handled missing values
* Converted categorical variables
* Standardized column formats

### Exploratory analysis

Focused on:

* Attrition rate across departments
* Salary vs attrition relationship
* Job satisfaction trends
* Overtime impact on employee turnover

### Feature analysis

Looked at how different variables relate to attrition and which ones seem most important.

---

## 5. Findings

### Salary matters

Employees with lower monthly income show a higher tendency to leave the company.

---

### Job satisfaction is a strong factor

Lower satisfaction levels are clearly linked with higher attrition.

---

### Overtime increases turnover

Employees working overtime regularly are more likely to resign.

---

### Department differences exist

Some departments have noticeably higher attrition rates compared to others.

---

## 6. Business insights

The analysis suggests that attrition is not random. It is influenced by a combination of salary, workload, and employee satisfaction.

Companies can reduce turnover by focusing on these areas.

---

## 7. Recommendations

* Improve salary structure for lower income groups
* Monitor job satisfaction regularly
* Review overtime workload policies
* Focus retention strategies on high-risk departments

---

## 8. Conclusion

This project shows how HR data can be used to understand employee behavior and reduce turnover.

Even simple analysis can provide useful insights for better decision-making in HR management.

* How attrition modeling works in practice
